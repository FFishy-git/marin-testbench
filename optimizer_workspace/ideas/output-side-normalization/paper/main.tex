\begin{abstract}
Muon-type optimizers orthogonalize the momentum of each weight matrix and, in
their preconditioned variants, whiten it against the second moment of the
layer's \emph{inputs}. The second moment of the layer's \emph{output cotangents}
is equally available and is not used. We add it, defining a family of
output-side normalized updates over a pinned 265M-parameter transformer recipe,
and run \nDistinctRuns{} full-budget training trajectories (2.68B tokens each,
eight A100s per run) to measure what it buys. Three results hold up. Output-side moments help
\emph{on top of} input whitening and not instead of it: added, the best geometry
improves held-out loss by \mlpdiagsel{} nats, while substituting output for
input normalization is \emph{worse} than either at \leftallsel{} nats. A matched
null control, which performs identical collection, communication and root work
with identity factors, recovers a fifth to a third of the apparent gain, so
reported improvements that omit such a control overstate geometry by that much.
And freshness dominates richness: diagonal output factors recomputed exactly
every update beat dense factors refreshed every four updates at half the step
overhead, while deleting the widest output factor entirely is worse than
diagonalizing it. The fourth result is cautionary and currently governs the
others. The effect is small next to the baseline's own tuning headroom:
tuning MuonT's learning rate alone improves the same metric by
\lrMuontGainFromTuning{} nats, \lrHeadroomRatio{} times the largest output-side
gain at the recipe rate, and learning rates selected on a short schedule do not
transfer, turning two of three geometries from winners into
\transferWorst{}-nat losers at full budget. No arm has shown a wall-clock win at
any loss target. We therefore present output-side normalization as a measured,
reproducible, and still-unpriced effect, and specify the small set of
experiments --- a null at the selected rate, a full-budget learning-rate
neighborhood, one freshness ablation, and checkpoint-only mechanistic probes ---
that would settle whether it is worth its cost.
\end{abstract}
