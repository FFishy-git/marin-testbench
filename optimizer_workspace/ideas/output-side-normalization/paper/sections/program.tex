\subsection{Why full-budget confirmation centered on \texorpdfstring{$0.0226$}{0.0226} rather than \texorpdfstring{$0.064$}{0.064}}

Section~\ref{sec:tuning} showed that $\eta = 0.064$ degraded full-budget
\muont{} by \waveTwelveMuontSelDiff{} nats relative to $0.0226$. Bracketing the
completed $0.0226$ anchor with $\{0.016, 0.032\}$ in Phase~1 directly confirmed
that the full-budget optimum sits inside $[0.016, 0.0226]$ for every arm
(Figure~\ref{fig:confirmation}a), avoiding Wave~12's over-stepped regime.

\subsection{Remaining offline curvature check}

While Table~\ref{tab:motivation} and Section~\ref{sec:mech-graft} settled
covariance structure and direction norms, one theoretical probe remains available
at zero training cost: testing whether $B \otimes C$ acts as a Kronecker
curvature model here. On a reserved token pool, form fresh empirical moments
$\widehat C = N^{-1}\sum_j x_j x_j^{\Tr}$ and
$\widehat B = N^{-1}\sum_j \delta_j \delta_j^{\Tr}$ and compare the true
empirical quadratic $N^{-1}\sum_j (\delta_j^{\Tr} D x_j)^2$ against the factored
trace $\tr(\widehat B D \widehat C D^{\Tr})$ across unit-Frobenius directions
$D$. Agreement in directional rankings would license a curvature interpretation;
disagreement would localize cross-token coupling without altering the
coordinate-alignment mechanism established in Section~\ref{sec:mot-aniso}.

\subsection{What we would not run}
\label{sec:nonprogram}

Three large bodies of work remain deliberately out of scope until Item~1 closes.

\paragraph{The full-model Newton--Schulz budget sweep.}
Fewer Newton--Schulz steps is a genuine efficiency lever: our numerical study
finds seven steps matching ten on cosine to a partial polar factor
($0.980$ against $0.979$) with 30\% fewer matrix products, and five steps still
at $0.951$ (Appendix~\ref{app:numerics}). Crossing three step budgets and two
precisions with every geometry reserves 54 configurations ($\approx 145\text{B}$
tokens) to test a lever shared equally by \muont{} and every candidate. If
pursued, it belongs first as a three-point baseline-only check.

\paragraph{Outer-only diagonal variants and combinatorial factorials.}
Wave~10 showed that dropping the pre-$\Nten$ inner factor $L$ (\outerdiag{} and
\fused{}) leaves only $-0.00044$ nats of net-of-null selection gain while saving
under $1\%$ step time compared with \texttt{inner\_\allowbreak outer\_\allowbreak leftdiag}.
Pre-orthogonalization scaling $L$ carries the geometric signal; post-orthogonalization $\hat L$ alone
does not warrant further full-budget slots. Likewise, randomized-basis
information controls and direction/decay factorials are deferred until Item~1
establishes the tuned margin.

\paragraph{Scaling.}
Larger batches and larger models wait on Item~1 and Item~4. At $d_{\text{model}}
> 512$, the cost advantage of width-thresholding grows cubicly with expansion
width, making larger scale the natural next step once full-budget tuned
efficiency is confirmed at 265M.

\subsection{Decision rules, set before the runs}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Attribution.} Every geometry claim is reported as candidate minus
  \emph{same-wave, same-rate} matched null (Item~2), never as candidate minus
  baseline alone.
\item \textbf{Quality.} A tuned quality claim requires the full-budget
  optimal-rate margin of Item~1 to exceed the Item~2 null artifact and to
  replicate across the reserved seeds 3/4/5 of Item~4 on the fixed external C4
  set.
\item \textbf{Efficiency.} A confirmatory wall-clock efficiency claim requires
  replication across reserved seeds 3/4/5 (Item~4) using a predeclared loss
  target, a launch-anchored clock covering compilation, training, evaluation, and
  checkpointing on uncontended eight-A100 allocations, and explicit censoring
  where a target is unreached.
\end{itemize}
