\section{Method and systematic arm taxonomy}
\label{sec:method}

Section~\ref{sec:mot-consequences} derived three structural requirements from the
baseline \taxid{MuonT} (\muont{}) trajectory: retain dense width-$512$ input
whitening, threshold output preconditioning by projection width (dense at
$d=512$, exact elementwise diagonal at $d=2048$, testing both dense and diagonal
\texttt{in:down} at $d=2048$), and lag output factors by one update on an
asynchronous side stream. Figure~\ref{fig:method_arch} illustrates both the
spatial assignment across a transformer layer and the asynchronous
one-step-lagged pipeline (\texttt{fast\_lag}). We now specify how the output
moments are observed and sharded across eight GPUs, and establish a formal
five-slot operator taxonomy covering every candidate, null control, and ablation
evaluated in the study.

\subsection{Where the output statistics come from}

The transformer we train has 32 layers and seven two-dimensional block matrices
per layer --- separate $q,k,v,o$ attention projections and a SwiGLU
$\text{gate},\text{up},\text{down}$ triple --- for 224 matrices under the matrix
optimizer. Embeddings, the output head and RMSNorm weights stay under a fused
AdamW group and are untouched by every arm.

Table~\ref{tab:projections} summarizes the exact shapes, aspect-ratio factors
$s$, and backward observation points for all seven projections. Because $q,k,v$
consume identical pre-attention activations and $\text{gate},\text{up}$ consume
identical pre-MLP activations, the input side shares moments down to four per
layer, only one of which is at width 2048. Every projection receives a distinct
backward cotangent and can share nothing, requiring seven output moments per
layer, two of which (\texttt{gate}, \texttt{up}) sit at width 2048.

Cotangents are sampled at the same 286 positions per sequence as the input
statistics, giving $N_t=36{,}608$ samples per update pooled across all
microbatches and all eight ranks, with EMA coefficient $\beta=0.938$ --- an
effective averaging window of about 16 updates. Raw FP32 sums and counts pool
\emph{before} the EMA, so the statistic is a globally pooled quantity and not a
per-rank one. Because any positive constant scale cancels from the trace
normalization in \eqref{eq:factors}, the capture applies a convenient power-of-two
scale rather than the exact loss-averaging constant; this leaves every factor
unchanged and is the reason diagnostics that report $B$ in summed-loss units
must rescale.

Output moments, their roots and the corresponding momentum are sharded by
contiguous layer ownership, four layers per rank. Per-update, each rank
accumulates replicated packed Gram partials, a \verb|reduce_scatter_tensor|
delivers globally pooled sums to each layer's owner, the owner computes its
roots and its four matrices' updates, and parameter deltas are all-gathered so
every replica applies all 224 updates. The output statistics run in a second
instance of the root engine with its own CUDA side stream, NCCL communicator
and host refresh pool. All of this is work the baseline does not do, which is
precisely why Section~\ref{sec:null} insists on a control that does it too.

\subsection{Operator signature and six taxonomic families}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

Every executed optimizer configuration in \eqref{eq:family} is uniquely
specified by a structured five-slot operator tuple
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tuple}
\opcfg{R_{512}}{R_{2048}}{L_{512}}{L_{2048}}{\tau},
\end{equation}
indexing:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R_{512}$: right-input operator on width-$512$ activations
  (\texttt{in:attn} for $q,k,v$, \texttt{in:o}, and \texttt{in:mlp} for
  $\text{gate},\text{up}$);
\item $R_{2048}$: right-input operator on post-SwiGLU width-$2048$ activations
  (\texttt{in:down});
\item $L_{512}$: left-cotangent operator on width-$512$ outputs
  (\texttt{out:q}, \texttt{out:k}, \texttt{out:v}, \texttt{out:o},
  \texttt{out:down});
\item $L_{2048}$: left-cotangent operator on pre-SwiGLU width-$2048$ outputs
  (\texttt{out:gate}, \texttt{out:up});
\item $\tau \in \{0, 1, \text{---}\}$: output-factor staleness in updates
  ($\tau=0$ synchronous current-update, $\tau=1$ asynchronous one-step lagged,
  $\text{---}$ when no output pipeline executes).
\end{enumerate}
Each spatial slot takes one of five operator primitives:
$\fFull$ for full spectral roots $(\In_d,\Out_d)$ via the cached-eigenbasis
solver;
$\fDiag$ for exact, age-zero elementwise diagonal roots
$(\In_d^{\diag},\Out_d^{\diag})$;
$\fOuter$ for post-$\Nten$ outer scaling $\Out_d$ only ($L=I$, written
$\fOuterFull$ when dense and $\fOuterDiag$ when diagonal);
$\fNone$ for uncollected identity factors; and
$\fNull$ for a computed-and-withheld matched null ($\fNullFull$ or $\fNullDiag$,
running the full capture, reduction, and root pipeline before forcing
$L=\hat L=I$). Superscript $(\cdot)^{\text{dn}}$ restricts an operator to the
$32$ \texttt{down} matrices ($12.6\%$ of weights).

Table~\ref{tab:arms} organizes all \nOptimizerArms{} configurations into six
taxonomic families that structure the tables and figures throughout the rest of
the paper:
\textbf{I.\ Baselines (\texttt{Ref})}, pairing unwhitened \taxid{Muon} with
input-whitened \taxid{MuonT};
\textbf{II.\ Width-Thresholded Two-Sided (\texttt{WT})}, our proposed core
family forming a complete $2\times 2$ factorial across asymmetric output
width-thresholding ($\opcfg{\fFull}{\fFull}{\fFull}{\fDiag}{\tau}$:
\taxid{WT-MLP}, \taxid{WT-MLP-Lag}) and symmetric MLP width-thresholding
($\opcfg{\fFull}{\fDiag}{\fFull}{\fDiag}{\tau}$: \taxid{WT-Sym},
\taxid{WT-Sym-Lag}) at $\tau\in\{0,1\}$;
\textbf{III.\ Pure-Diagonal Output (\texttt{OD})}, the zero-eigendecomposition
limit across all seven outputs (\taxid{OD-All}) or outer-only (\taxid{OD-Outer});
\textbf{IV.\ Dense Two-Sided Ceiling (\texttt{DT})}, applying dense spectral
roots to all $224$ outputs at $\tau=0$ (\taxid{DT-All}) and $\tau=1$
(\taxid{DT-All-Lag});
\textbf{V.\ Matched Execution-Path Nulls (\texttt{Null})}, isolating numerical
reassociation and host-thread contention from geometry (\taxid{Null-WT},
\taxid{Null-Dense}, \taxid{Null-OD}); and
\textbf{VI.\ Structural \& Input-Degradation Ablations (\texttt{Abl})}, testing
scope truncation (\taxid{Abl-No2048}, \taxid{Abl-OuterDense},
\taxid{Abl-DownOnly}) and input removal or all-width diagonalization
(\taxid{Abl-OutOnly}, \taxid{Abl-BothDiag}, \taxid{Abl-DownSwap}). Full update
equations appear in Appendix~\ref{app:arms}.

\subsection{Two controls that the design forces}
\label{sec:method-controls}

\paragraph{The matched null.}
Adding output factors to this implementation changes the numerical path for
every block matrix: the baseline uses a batched update with fused
direction/decay products and split-precision root products, while the
output-side arms use an owned-layer path. Momentum is bitwise identical, but the
applied delta differs by floating-point reassociation, and the host refresh
schedule changes because two root engines now share host worker threads. A loss
difference between an output-side arm and the baseline therefore confounds
geometry with execution.

The matched null resolves this. It runs the candidate's entire code path ---
cotangent capture, pooled moments, communication, root computation, host
refresh, scheduling, checkpoint state --- and then forces $L=\hat L=I$. Its
target is the baseline's mathematics via the candidate's execution. Candidate
minus null isolates geometry; null minus baseline measures the path.
Section~\ref{sec:null} reports that the second quantity is not small.

\paragraph{The latency variant.}
Output rows exist only after the backward pass, so synchronous ($\tau=0$)
execution launches output statistics and roots after the last microbatch's
backward and waits for them inside the same update. That wait sits on the
critical path. The lagged arms ($\tau=1$) apply factors computed from the
\emph{previous} update's rows, moving collection to just before the next launch
and giving the work a full update plus a forward and backward to complete. At
$\beta=0.938$ a one-update lag is about one-sixteenth of the averaging window,
far less staleness than Shampoo-family methods accept, and comparing $\tau=0$
against $\tau=1$ on both asymmetric (\taxid{WT-MLP} vs.\ \taxid{WT-MLP-Lag}) and
symmetric (\taxid{WT-Sym} vs.\ \taxid{WT-Sym-Lag}) width-thresholding separates
staleness from spatial geometry.

\subsection{Where the cost is}
\label{sec:method-cost}

The output side's expense is concentrated and predictable. Of the 224 output
statistics, 160 are at width 512 and 64 at width 2048. Dense root work scales as
$d^3$ and Gram accumulation as $d^2$, so the two MLP-expansion projections carry
96.2\% of the eigendecomposition work and 86.5\% of the Gram work while being
29\% of the statistics. They hold 0.500 of the 0.579\,GiB of packed Gram
partials and 1.000 of the 1.156\,GiB of cached eigenbases. Per rank, an arm with
dense output factors on all blocks adds roughly 0.58\,GiB of replicated Gram
partials, an equal \verb|reduce_scatter| payload per update, about 1.8\,GiB of
BF16 sampled-row buffers, and 0.75\,GiB of owned output state. On the input
side, the 32 width-2048 \texttt{in:down} statistics similarly contribute
$268.6$\,MB of packed \verb|reduce_scatter| volume per update and four
$2048\times 2048$ cached eigenbases per rank.

This is why our taxonomy spans four progressive cost tiers across the width-2048
projections: \taxid{DT-All} keeps all width-2048 sides dense, \taxid{WT-MLP}
diagonalizes the two width-2048 outputs, \taxid{WT-Sym} diagonalizes
\emph{both} width-2048 MLP sides (\texttt{out:gate,up} and \texttt{in:down})
while keeping all width-512 sides dense, and \taxid{OD-All} diagonalizes all
seven outputs to eliminate output eigendecompositions altogether.
